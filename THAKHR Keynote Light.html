The delay

Unclear claims enter the clarification loop — returned, re-submitted, re-queued. Each round trip adds days.

Three root inefficiencies

Manual handling · data fragmentation · analytical blindness. Rich data, almost no prediction (Kraus et al., 2021).

The member

Not in this diagram at all. She learns of a rejection at the pharmacy counter, then calls to ask why. No status. No reason.

Every design decision that follows starts from that counter. Reem will show you what we built.

Read the same three problems from the other side

Every inefficiency is a disruption opportunity.

Inefficiency

Manual claim handling

Opportunity

Exactly what modern AI does well — read semi-structured documents, apply rules, flag variance (Davenport & Kalakota, 2019; Secinaro et al., 2021).

Inefficiency

Data fragmentation

Opportunity

One member-and-insurance layer binding the existing systems — each party sees only what its role permits.

Inefficiency

Analytical blindness

Opportunity

Predictive modelling across the population — catch diabetic, cardiac and hypertension risk early, and act preventively.

Our design position: the transformation is preservative, not destructive. We add an intelligent layer on top of what the UAE already built — not a replacement.

Justification for technology selection

We chose AI — and we can show what we chose against.

Blockchain — immutable record, but reads and decides nothing. Integration weight we don't need yet.
IoT & wearables — a real future upgrade to Health Radar, not needed to prove the idea.
Cloud — everything runs on it, so it's infrastructure, not the disruption.
AI agent + predictive analytics — a claim is language, context and judgement. Exactly where medical AI has matured (Rajpurkar et al., 2022; Topol, 2019).

Stakeholder analysis & prioritisation

Eight groups want eight things. Three techniques turn that into one ordered list.

Sponsors · returnAdjusters · changed workHospitals · fast settlement Government · auditability & consent = entry conditionsMembers · status & reasonsEmployers · no complaints
Technique 01

MoSCoW

Forces a real choice about the minimum usable release (Cobb, 2023). Must: intake, member visibility, auto-adjudication. Won't (this release): personalised prevention — the part that protects scope.

Technique 02

WSJF

Cost of delay ÷ job size (Leffingwell, 2018). Intake scores 9.0. Fraud alerts score 8.3 — but sequenced after Dakhtar, because fraud models can't learn without intake history. Dependencies bend the economics.

Technique 03

Value vs Effort

A cross-check. Quick wins: intake & visibility. Strategic bets: Dakhtar & fraud. Where it disagreed with WSJF, we returned to stakeholders — not the bigger number.

An honest critique — three properties, each can fail

Single

True only while it's the only list. The real risk: stakeholders keeping private spreadsheets on the side.

Prioritised

Never better than its estimates — and our WSJF scoring is subjective and uncalibrated.

Dynamic

Becomes instability if reordered too often. Responsiveness and thrash look identical from outside.

The artifact doesn't solve these — it makes them visible. A more modest claim than the literature makes, and the one we can defend.

This is Water-Scrum-Fall (West, 2011) — the balance of agility and discipline (Boehm & Turner, 2004). Agility within the constraints, not against them.

Fostering trust and learning

On a project that automates decisions, psychological safety is not a soft topic.

Trust is built by practice, not declaration — the Daily Scrum does most of the work, because visible blockers can't fester (Denning, 2018; Layton et al., 2020).

The change we won't dress up

Adjusters will fear automation. Leadership must say it honestly: Dakhtar removes routine work, not jobs — backed by retraining toward investigation.

Conflict · a four-step framework
01 Define the problem — separate it from the people.
02 Hear everyone — active listening before deciding.
03 Find common ground — usually a shared wish to ship this Sprint.
04 Solve it together — win–win, Scrum Master mediating.

When facilitation runs out: the Product Owner decides priority, the developers decide implementation. Self-organisation completes it — the people doing the work own the plan.

Conclusions · what this project found
Finding 01

The problem is concentrated

At the manual provider–insurer interface — the source of the 50.4-hour turnaround.

Finding 02

AI is the right technology

Adjudication is language and judgement work AI can now do under oversight.

Finding 03 · least intuitive

Delivery matters as much as tech

Not pure Agile — a hybrid with compliance gates (Rigby et al., 2016).

The honest reading of the KPI cards

Yearly averages look like a miss — 50.4 hrs vs 24, 2.1% fraud vs 2.5% — because they include the manual months. December is the real steady state: ≈8 hrs, 79% auto-approval, fraud above target. The gap isn't a weakness — it is the measurement of the change itself.

Limitations — stated plainly

The dataset is a working model · it assumes cooperation we couldn't test · model bias & accountability deserve deeper treatment. We'd rather name them than have you find them.
